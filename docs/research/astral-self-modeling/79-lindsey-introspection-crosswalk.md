## Controls to retain

The paper's design reinforces these controls in Astral protocols:

1. Keep activation and no-intervention prompts byte-identical.
2. Include unrelated concept injections, not only concept-matched injections.
3. Test temporal order, including injection-before-report and injection-after-
   output controls where the protocol permits it.
4. Score the first report decision separately from later narrative details.
5. Treat additional phenomenological or mechanistic language as unverified
   unless it has an independent target and held-out validation.

These controls do not authorize a new run. They are protocol-review inputs for a
future separately authorized fresh actor and instrument.

## Claim separation

The paper supports a bounded literature statement: some tested models can,
under an unnatural activation-injection setup, produce reports that are
causally sensitive to certain manipulated internal representations. The paper
also states that the behavior is unreliable, that the underlying mechanism is
not identified, and that metacognitive representations are not directly shown.

Astral asks a different primary question: whether privileged telemetry predicts
directly measured intervention effects on held-out actor seeds and prompt
families better than activation-only, text-only, and capacity-matched controls.
Therefore:

- paper self-report results are relevant prior art and secondary diagnostics;
- they do not establish Astral's causal-effect prediction objective;
- a positive Astral report gap remains information-presence evidence only;
- neither result establishes consciousness, global introspection, semantic
  correctness, or faithful self-explanation.
